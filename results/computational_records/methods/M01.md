## Text S1. UCMR Data, Reporting Levels, Event Construction, and Cross-Cycle Matching

The UCMR 3 and UCMR 5 occurrence archives were obtained from EPA.<sup>1,2</sup> UCMR 3 covers sampling years 2013–2015. All primary UCMR 5 cohorts, labels, folds, estimates, and figures were generated from the final occurrence archive downloaded on September 13, 2026. Source bytes, file size, and digest are reported in Text S7. The primary cohort used sample event 1 (SE1), the first scheduled sampling period available across source-water categories, and the six PFAS measured in both cycles: perfluorobutanesulfonic acid (PFBS), perfluoroheptanoic acid (PFHpA), perfluorohexanesulfonic acid (PFHxS), perfluorononanoic acid (PFNA), perfluorooctanoic acid (PFOA), and perfluorooctanesulfonic acid (PFOS). For each analyte, *reported* denotes a numeric result at or above the applicable program minimum reporting level (MRL). It does not denote exposure, regulatory noncompliance, or a health-risk endpoint.

All 46,062 cross-cycle analyte pairs used EPA Method 537 Revision 1.1 in UCMR 3 and EPA Method 533 in UCMR 5; the UCMR 3 occurrence archive shortens the former label to Method 537. Method 537 Revision 1.1 specifies a 250 mL sample, solid-phase extraction, surrogate standards, internal-standard quantitation, and liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry.<sup>3</sup> Method 533 permits 100–250 mL samples and uses isotope-dilution analogues, anion-exchange solid-phase extraction, isotope-dilution quantitation, and liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry.<sup>4</sup> Applying UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs to both cycles harmonizes the numeric reporting thresholds, but it does not make the analytical procedures equivalent. Table S2 lists the methods and analyte-specific reporting levels.

A strict sample-collection event was keyed by public water system identifier (PWSID), facility identifier, sample-point identifier, collection date, and sample identifier. Within each administrative sampling location and cycle, an event was retained only when each of the six shared PFAS occurred exactly once and no additional shared-PFAS SE1 event occurred at that location. This rule produced 15,011 UCMR 3 events and 26,461 UCMR 5 events (Table S1). A location-only construction retained one additional UCMR 3 panel and 20 additional UCMR 5 panels; 17 of the additional UCMR 5 panels combined multiple dates, and all 20 combined multiple sample identifiers. These panels were excluded by the strict event rule.

Cross-cycle pairs were formed on PWSID, facility identifier, and sample-point identifier after strict within-cycle event construction. Each retained location contributed one complete event per cycle. The matched cohort comprised 7,677 location pairs, 2,421 public water systems (PWSs), and 57 UCMR reporting-jurisdiction strata, as encoded by the archive's State field. Every matched event was coded as an entry point to a distribution system and therefore represents drinking water sampled before distribution, not source water or water at a household tap. Of these pairs, 7,673 were classified as large systems in both cycles; four changed from large to small. Matching was administrative: unchanged intake geometry, treatment train, source-water contribution, and physical sampling point were not verified. A retained event is therefore neither an annual PWS exposure nor a resident-level burden measure. The matched locations comprised 51.142% of strict UCMR 3 events and 29.013% of strict UCMR 5 events. Native-cutoff $K_6\ge2$ rates were 0.469% and 1.186% in matched and unmatched UCMR 3 events, respectively, and 10.798% and 6.261% in matched and unmatched UCMR 5 events (Table S5). These are unweighted, within-cycle descriptive rates; unmatched events were not cross-cycle pairs and do not provide a counterfactual estimate for the matched cohort. No inverse-probability correction was applied.
