## Text S21. Selective Reliability of Frozen Joint Predictions

This exploratory diagnostic distinguished improvement of a predictive distribution from reliable identification of an individual reporting pattern. The archived four outer folds and sixteen interior threshold queries were retained. Native-limit probabilities at $α = 0$ were additionally replayed from the same fixed marginal predictions and joint parameters, giving 21,156 native-limit rows separately from the 338,496 interior-query rows. No neural or dependence model was retrained. The empirical-composition reference used its original permitted training folds and fixed smoothing rule; its native-limit table was computed by that same rule. This reference is not constrained to preserve the neural marginal probabilities.

For each probability vector, the point prediction was the state with maximum probability, with deterministic state-code ordering for exact ties. Its maximum probability defined confidence. Fixed acceptance rules required confidence of at least 0.80, 0.90, 0.95, or 0.99. No rule was selected from held-out correctness. Count predictions instead maximized the summed probabilities for each count and used their own confidence. Exact-pattern error, count error, and identity error conditional on the exact-pattern prediction having the correct count were recorded separately. The latter condition is not the same as a correct modal count prediction. Results were stratified by predicted zero/nonzero state and observed zero/one/multiple reports. Primary summaries gave equal weight to jurisdictions, PWSs, locations, and threshold queries successively; event-equal summaries were secondary.

Matched-coverage diagnostics ranked confidence separately within each fold and query at 25%, 50%, 75%, 90%, and 100% weighted coverage. Ties used a fixed hash of the location identifier rather than outcomes. The final accepted record could slightly exceed the requested weight. These curves use evaluation confidence ranks and are diagnostic comparisons, not learned deployment thresholds. A separate rule chose acceptance thresholds from the corresponding outer-training records at native limits, using the outer-test-clean inner-cross-fitted marginal predictions and fixed joint parameters. Quantiles targeted 25%, 50%, 75%, and 90% training coverage. Large ties were retained rather than split by test outcomes, so achieved coverage could differ greatly from the nominal value. These are training-selected rules, not independently calibrated risk guarantees; the joint parameters were fitted on those training records.

For native-limit fixed rules and the 50% training-coverage rule, 2,000 paired two-level jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap draws were applied to full-cohort error numerators and acceptance denominators, with an independent PWS resample for each sampled jurisdiction copy. Conditional risks were ratios of the resampled sums. Empty subgroup denominators yielded undefined draws rather than zero risk. Finite-draw counts accompany every interval. Very small accepted subgroups, including a single nonzero prediction, do not support a reliability guarantee even when their empirical bootstrap interval is degenerate. Intervals retain the original post-selection and fixed-model limitations.

At confidence 0.95, shared shock accepted 378 native-limit locations and Gaussian factor 2 accepted 309; 377 and 308 of their respective point predictions were zero reports. Primary-weighted coverage was 6.710% and 5.257%, and exact-pattern error was 1.681% and 1.452%. Independence accepted 131 locations, including 130 zero predictions; the empirical-composition reference accepted only one location. No neural-marginal model accepted a native-limit prediction at confidence 0.99. At approximately 50% matched weighted coverage, exact-pattern errors were 5.859%, 5.954%, 5.977%, and 11.731% for independence, shared shock, Gaussian factor 2, and empirical composition, respectively (archived matched-coverage diagnostics). Table S38 instead reports fixed-confidence rules. These findings do not negate gains in proper distribution scores. They show that those gains did not translate into a comparably broad set of confidently identified nonzero combinations.

Record keys, native-limit probability vectors, confidence bins, complete risk-coverage data, training-only thresholds, subgroup denominators, and event-equal sensitivity are archived. Replacement of all outer-test and reserved-validation target fields left the training-policy inputs unchanged. An independent evaluator reconstructed native point predictions, count predictions, confidence rules, and hierarchical weights from raw probabilities. The native endpoint was kept outside the original sixteen-query integrated estimand.
