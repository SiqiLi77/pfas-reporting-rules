## Text S16. Complete Subsequent-Pattern Forecasting and Interval Sensitivity

Text S14 conditions on the observed next-sample count. The present extension removed that requirement by estimating a probability for every count from zero to 25 using only initial information. For subsequent reported set S and initial information x, the full predictive distribution was defined as

\[
P(S\mid x)=r_k(x)q_k(S\mid x),\qquad k=|S|.
\]

Here $r_k(x)$ is the probability of count k and $q_k(S\mid x)$ is the normalized conditional Bernoulli probability of set S given that count, as defined in Text S14. The primary extension combined ridge-penalized softmax count probabilities with the previously frozen own-history conditional-identity model. Initial water type, system size, sampling-month sine and cosine, initial report count, and permitted own-analyte report histories supplied the inputs. Future counts, dates, elapsed intervals, concentrations, and identifiers were not predictors. Complete-pattern log loss separates exactly into count log loss and conditional-identity log loss. Forecast scores average equally over all evaluation locations, including counts of zero and 25; this differs from the informative-count denominator in Text S14. Probabilities of multiple reports and of any subsequent reported analyte absent from the initial reported set were obtained by summing the fitted distribution. A newly reported analyte does not establish new contamination onset.

Five existing jurisdiction folds separated fitting, selection, and evaluation. For evaluation fold f, fold f + 1 modulo five supplied validation; the remaining three supplied initial fitting. Penalties were selected from 0.0001, 0.001, and 0.01, followed by refitting on all four non-evaluation folds. Count models used validation count loss, whereas independent references used complete-pattern loss. All 60 primary candidate fits and refits converged. A subsequent robustness grid added 0.00001. The stronger empirical count reference grouped initial counts at six or more, water type, and system size. Cell frequencies were shrunk toward training-global frequencies with 0.5 pseudocount per count category; shrinkage strength was selected from 10 and 50 using validation count loss. Empirical and softmax count models used the same conditional-identity component. The lower-ridge sensitivity was specified after the original forecast results and is reported alongside them rather than presented as the original primary analysis.

The retrospective calendar evaluation fitted only outcomes collected by December 31, 2024 and evaluated locations with initial samples collected from January 1, 2025 onward. Evaluation PWSs were excluded from all fitting records. There were 13,201 initial training and 3,333 validation locations, followed by refitting on all 16,534 eligible earlier records and evaluation at 5,387 later locations. The identity component was also refitted on earlier records, selecting its penalty from 0.01 and 0.1. Thus no component reused a later-trained identity fit. The analysis remains within an already examined archive, not external confirmation.

The original grid gave complete-pattern losses of 1.14341, 0.94111, and 0.79231 for independent site/count, independent own-history, and predicted-count/identity models. Table S32 reports the stronger-reference sensitivity. Against lower-ridge independence, blocked complete-pattern loss decreased by 0.14573 (95% interval, 0.12946–0.16269), whereas the conditional-identity reduction was −0.00196 (−0.00489 to 0.00099). Against empirical count transitions, the complete-pattern advantage narrowed to 0.00956 (0.00578–0.01327). The calendar-separated advantage over empirical transitions was 0.00966 (0.00277–0.01682). Additional count-Brier and newly-reported-analyte Brier benefits over that reference were unresolved in both evaluations. Calendar binary multi-report Brier improvement over independence also had an interval spanning zero. Table S32 separates mean total loss into count and identity components. These complete 25-analyte forecasts do not preserve the six marginal probabilities of the controlled-masking experiment and do not replace its binary result.

A further post-result sensitivity scored the saved predictions within intervals of 1–89, 90–270, 271–450, and more than 450 days, and after excluding intervals shorter than 90 days. No model was refitted or supplied the realized interval. The protocol fixed these groups before calculating their estimates. In the at-least-90-day subset, advantages over empirical count transitions were 0.00759 (0.00368–0.01154) in blocked evaluation and 0.00802 (0.00149–0.01492) in calendar evaluation (Table S34). Additional gains were unresolved in the 271–450-day groups. Only four calendar locations exceeded 450 days; their point estimates are descriptive and their degenerate resampling intervals are not used for inference. The subgroup results do not establish a fixed prediction horizon or performance at arbitrary future dates.

All reported forecast intervals use 2,000 paired PWS-cluster bootstrap draws within fixed jurisdictions, retain location-equal score aggregation, and condition on fitted predictions rather than the full model-development process. Independent prediction and scoring checks reproduced the estimates. Specifications, coefficients, transformations, tuning records, and detailed verification outputs are indexed in the computational supplement.
