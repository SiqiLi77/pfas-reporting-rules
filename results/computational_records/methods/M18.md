## Text S18. Concentration Resolution of the Panel-Induced Source-Water Contrast

This sensitivity was specified after inspection of the native-limit source-water results and before calculating the additional concentration-floor estimates. It is exploratory. Exactly the 20,162 supported locations, 7,911 PWSs, 34 jurisdictions, and 59 jurisdiction-by-system-size strata from Text S15 were retained. No subsequent sample was required. A full key comprising PWSID, FacilityID, SamplePointID, CollectionDate, and SampleID recovered 504,050 analyte rows from the final archive. All 25 reporting masks matched the existing first-sample table. Concentrations also agreed exactly with the first-sample measurements at the 19,617 locations available in the separate paired-sample cohort. Nondetects retained their censored status and were not imputed as zero.

The two panels were evaluated at native limits and at concentration floors c of 5, 10, 20, and 40 ng/L. Each analyte's cutoff was max(native MRL, c). At 5 and 10 ng/L these are floors, not identical analyte cutoffs, because the native NFDHA reporting limit is 20 ng/L. At 20 and 40 ng/L all 25 analytes have identical mass-concentration cutoffs. These values define reporting-resolution sensitivities rather than health-based thresholds, molar equivalence, or recommended monitoring limits. All conditions were retained, without selecting a favorable cutoff.

Original stratum weights and support were held fixed. For each panel and floor, source-specific multi-PFAS reporting rates were calculated before taking the surface-water-minus-groundwater contrast. The panel effect was the 25-analyte contrast minus the six-analyte contrast, equivalently the difference between source categories in newly multiple records after panel expansion. Intervals were recomputed jointly across conditions from 2,000 PWS-cluster resamples within fixed jurisdictions of the supported cohort. All 2,000 draws were finite. Native-limit point estimates match those in main-text Figure 3a–b. The 1,994 finite full-frame draws used there and the 2,000 supported-cohort draws used in Figure 3c give slightly different intervals; neither the support nor the panel-effect definition changes. Pointwise 95% intervals are shown in Table S35 and main-text Fig. 3c. For the four positive floors, a simultaneous band additionally used the 95th percentile of the maximum absolute centered bootstrap deviation divided by its condition-specific bootstrap standard deviation. This band addresses these four contrasts, not prior analytical selection or general population transportability.

The panel-induced source contrast declined from 4.640 percentage points at native limits to 2.986, 0.504, 0.134, and −0.020 points at the four successive floors. The simultaneous intervals were 2.167–3.806, 0.026–0.981, −0.142–0.411, and −0.141–0.100 points. The prespecified diagnostic for this amendment, native minus common-20 panel effect, was 4.506 points (pointwise 95% interval, 3.645–5.325). Attenuation localizes the concentration dependence of the reporting comparison; it does not imply that excluded low-concentration reports were false or environmentally irrelevant. Neither interval contains uncertainty from measurement error, administrative source classification, or sampling-frame selection.

Panel nesting, threshold-path monotonicity, point estimates, and saved interval calculations were checked independently. Verification details and the original source/archive identifiers are consolidated in the computational appendix.
