## Text S25. Environmental-contrast reconstruction and count-conditioned chemical resolution

This exploratory extension evaluated previously saved predictions after model development. Models, hyperparameters, inputs, and state probabilities were fixed before these diagnostics, but the data had already been examined. National evaluation intersected the 21,156 fixed-margin outer-fold events with the 26,423-event auxiliary cohort, retaining 21,134 events, 8,308 PWSs, and 56 jurisdictions. The excluded 22 events lacked eligible auxiliary panels. Event identities, labels, metadata, and folds were checked for agreement. Each prediction retained its original held-out status. Auxiliary five-fold fits and fixed-margin four-outer-fold fits have different training designs, so alignment of their evaluation samples does not isolate an architecture effect.

All endpoints in this section use the six native UCMR 5 numeric cutoffs (alpha = 0). This was a dependence-fitting threshold anchor, but the evaluated jurisdictions remained held out. It is not the original 16-query threshold-path average. The four frozen references were independence, shared shock, Gaussian factor-2, and training empirical composition. The latter has different margins. Six auxiliary conditions crossed independent logistic, linear count–identity, and nonlinear count–identity models with no auxiliary measurements or the selected three measurements. Washington used the previously saved 1,819-event current-metadata-proxy predictions from 1,456 PWSs and 39 counties. No local refitting or calibration was performed.

The four designated environmental endpoints were at least two reports, simultaneous reporting of at least one carboxylate and one sulfonate, PFOA–PFOS co-reporting, and PFHxS–PFOS co-reporting. Carboxylates were PFHpA, PFNA, and PFOA; sulfonates were PFBS, PFHxS, and PFOS. Pair endpoints permit any status of the remaining four analytes. These are not exact two-analyte patterns, source tracers, or treatment-efficiency endpoints. All 15 pair endpoints are retained in the machine-readable results, including unfavorable comparisons.

For each event and endpoint, the reconstructed probability was the sum of the 64-state probabilities satisfying the endpoint. No maximum-probability pattern, hard probability threshold, or calibration to evaluation group rates was used. Common support required at least 20 locations and five PWSs for each water-source category within a jurisdiction-by-system-size stratum. Eligibility used metadata rather than outcome status. Within each supported stratum and source category, indicators or probabilities were event-averaged. Both categories and every reporting condition then received the same pooled-location stratum weights. The primary quantity was the standardized surface-water rate minus the standardized groundwater rate. Bias was reconstructed minus measured; absolute-error reduction was reference absolute error minus candidate absolute error. Because the measured contrast can be near zero, no relative percentage-recovery measure was used. Group-rate biases were retained alongside contrast errors to expose cancellation.

National support comprised 15,723 events and 6,368 distinct PWSs in 51 strata and 29 jurisdictions. PWSs contributing multiple source categories were resampled together; category PWS counts therefore do not sum to the distinct total. The 5,000 bootstrap draws sampled PWSs within each fixed jurisdiction, retained all their selected events, and recomputed both group means and the paired contrast using fixed support and stratum weights. Two draws with an empty required source cell were omitted; 4,998 remained. These intervals condition on the fitted predictions, supported jurisdictions, and chosen population weights. Equal-jurisdiction weights and leave-one-jurisdiction point estimates are sensitivity analyses, not additional independent replications.

Washington source support contained 1,818 events: 1,776 groundwater and 42 surface-water events. One event was excluded by common-support rules. None of the 42 surface-water events was positive for any designated endpoint. The empirical bootstrap cannot represent an unobserved positive outcome in this group; its zero observed-rate interval does not imply that the population rate is zero. PWS-level intervals for this source comparison are conditional descriptive uncertainty, not evidence of a precisely established statewide source contrast. Current metadata may also differ from sampling-time conditions.

Chemical scores used all 21,134 national or 1,819 Washington aligned events, not only source-support events. National scores gave equal weight to jurisdictions, then PWSs, then events; Washington scores were PWS-equal. Binary endpoint Brier scores and four-state functional-group log loss were calculated directly. For conditional evaluation, each 64-state distribution was restricted and renormalized to states with the observed PFAS count. Functional-group log loss then scored neither group, carboxylates only, sulfonates only, or both. Pair log loss scored presence or absence of the named co-report within that count. The observed count entered only the diagnostic scoring operation and was never supplied during prediction. Count-determined endpoints contribute zero conditional loss. For pair endpoints, counts zero, one, and six determine the outcome; for functional-group states, counts zero, four, five, and six do so. Scores retain all events. Table S46 reports both all-event identity loss and a separate “Identity, count 1–5” diagnostic; only the latter uses the restricted denominator. Washington PWS-equal weighting is unchanged.

Conditional probabilities used the same normalized distribution for numerator and denominator, after adding $10^{-12}$ total uniform probability mass across the 64 states. Checks covered state sums, retained visible reports, fixed-margin agreement, and the pattern = count + identity decomposition. The Washington logistic identity gain of 0.114924 on the count-one-to-five denominator was reproduced. Endpoint support by observed count and detailed numerical checks are indexed in the computational supplement.

Paired score intervals used 5,000 PWS draws within national jurisdictions or Washington PWS draws. A county-cluster Washington sensitivity resampled county totals of PWS means and divided by the corresponding PWS count, retaining the original PWS-equal estimand. For each model comparison, the three designated conditional-chemical gains received a joint 95% interval based on the maximum absolute standardized, centered bootstrap deviation. This controls the displayed three-endpoint family, not every model and all exploratory pairs globally. No endpoint or candidate was selected from these results. Confidence intervals remain conditional on development and saved predictions.

The native-endpoint binary gain does not replace the threshold-path result: shared shock reduced Brier loss by 0.001431 under fixed-jurisdiction resampling at alpha = 0, whereas the original 16-query comparison used two-level resampling and showed no resolved average binary gain. Similarly, the national shared-shock contrast-error increase was 0.50 percentage points under pooled-location stratum weights, persisted under deletion of each jurisdiction, but did not persist under equal-jurisdiction weights. These findings constrain inference to the specified target and population definition. Models did not reconstruct the omitted 19-analyte panel or historical below-limit concentrations.

The marginal diagnostic used the same 15,723 source-supported events, native cutoffs, and pooled-location stratum weights, retaining all six analytes. Marginal predictions were obtained by summing the frozen 64-state distributions, and observed rates used native-limit indicators. Expected count was the sum of the six marginal probabilities, checked against the count distribution. Table S55 reports pointwise intervals for predicted-minus-observed group biases, using the same 4,998 estimable PWS-bootstrap draws. These are group-average probability errors, not calibration across individual prediction ranges. No test-group recalibration was performed.

The count-prior diagnostic used archived training count probabilities at the native-limit anchor for each outer fold. Probabilities were spread uniformly over patterns of each count, then projected to the original six margins using the existing iterative-proportional-fitting rule. No parameters were re-estimated. The earlier threshold-order violation remains relevant to its interpretation as a querywise benchmark (Text S11). Table S56 reports source-group rates, biases, and paired contrast errors; count spectra and the other designated endpoints remain available in the computational supplement.

The observed source-group mean counts were 0.291845 and 0.328869 reports per event (groundwater and surface water), whereas all four fixed-margin rules produced 0.339216 and 0.258552. Fixed margins therefore preserved an asymmetric group-average error before the choice of dependence model. Shared shock reduced the predicted single-report fractions from 22.16% to 6.78% in groundwater systems and from 18.75% to 6.41% in surface-water systems; observed fractions were 5.17% and 8.57%. Probability moved toward zero and multiple reports while the means remained fixed. These identities locate distinct marginal and allocation discrepancies but do not identify a unique causal decomposition of the multi-PFAS contrast error.

In main-text Figure 6b, the origin denotes zero group-rate bias and the equal-bias diagonal denotes zero signed contrast error. The empirical-composition reference reduced primary contrast error; equal-jurisdiction weights yielded similar measured and shared-shock contrasts (Tables S51 and S54). Those weights define a different population mixture and were not selected to improve performance. Table S57 reports Washington PFHxS–PFOS support, scores, rates, and paired contrast errors. With auxiliary measurements, both group-rate point estimates exceeded the observations; their biases partly canceled in the contrast. The groundwater bias interval included zero. Accurate contrasts can therefore coexist with inaccurate group-rate estimates.
