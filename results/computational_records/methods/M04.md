## Text S4. Controlled Threshold Masking and Marginal Prediction

The predictive cohort contained 26,461 complete six-PFAS UCMR 5 events. Native-MRL measurements supplied labels after inputs were masked once at the UCMR 3 cutoffs; the information set was not remasked at each query threshold. For each location, the fixed old-cutoff information set contained sampling month, region, system-size category, water-source category, a count of PFAS visible at the old cutoffs, and analyte-token fields indicating whether each PFAS was visible at its UCMR 3 cutoff and, if visible, its log concentration relative to that cutoff. For a PFAS hidden by the old cutoff, both visibility and log-ratio inputs were fixed at zero regardless of the specific modern sub-cutoff measurement. Marginal-model comparisons scored only analyte–location pairs hidden by an older cutoff; joint-distribution comparisons used all six targets. Other PFAS in the same event could contribute only through measurements visible at their UCMR 3 cutoffs; the target PFAS modern result below the old cutoff served only as its label. The prediction target is conditional panel completion from information retained under older reporting limits, not pre-sampling occurrence at a previously unmeasured location. Structural occurrence and historical concentration distributions are not estimated.

The masking audit covered all 158,569 old-cutoff-hidden analyte–location pairs across the five folds. Replacing the modern detection indicator, hidden-outcome label, and modern log concentration for each target left both the Transformer-token and endpoint-model flat inputs exactly unchanged. The maximum absolute input difference and number of changed input elements were 0 for both preprocessors (Table S9). The audit verifies the implemented information barrier, not predictive performance on independent data.

Let $X_i^{(3)}$ denote the information retained after the six-analyte panel was masked once at the UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs. For analyte $j$, let $V_{ij}=1$ when its measured UCMR 5 concentration was visible at the UCMR 3 cutoff $L_{3j}$. Because every queried threshold lay between the UCMR 5 native MRL $L_{5j}$ and $L_{3j}$, a visible analyte had reporting probability one conditional on $X_i^{(3)}$; this value was determined by the retained observation rather than predicted by the model. For an analyte hidden at $L_{3j}$, the calibrated hidden native-reporting hurdle was

\[
h_{ij}=P(L_{5j}\le C_{ij}<L_{3j}\mid V_{ij}=0,X_i^{(3)}).
\]

This probability represents native-MRL reporting within the old-cutoff-hidden risk set. It is not the structural occurrence probability $P(C_{ij}>0)$.

On the log-concentration scale, the threshold path for analyte $j$ was

\[
\ell_j(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)\ell_{5j}+\alpha\ell_{3j},
\qquad \ell_{tj}=\log L_{tj},
\qquad L_j(\alpha)=\exp\{\ell_j(\alpha)\},
\qquad 0\le\alpha\le1.
\]

Conditional on a hidden native-MRL report, $Z_{ij}=\log C_{ij}$ followed a normal distribution with mean $\mu_{ij}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{ij}$, truncated to $[\ell_{5j},\ell_{3j})$. Let $F_{ij}$ denote the corresponding untruncated normal cumulative distribution function. The conditional probability of exceeding a queried threshold within that truncated interval was

\[
q_{ij}(\alpha)=
\frac{F_{ij}(\ell_{3j})-F_{ij}\{\ell_j(\alpha)\}}
{F_{ij}(\ell_{3j})-F_{ij}(\ell_{5j})}.
\]

The marginal reporting probability supplied to the joint models was

\[
p_{ij}(\alpha)=V_{ij}+(1-V_{ij})h_{ij}q_{ij}(\alpha).
\]

For an old-cutoff-hidden analyte, $p_{ij}(0)=h_{ij}$ and $p_{ij}(1)=0$; for an analyte already visible at the old cutoff, $p_{ij}(\alpha)=1$ throughout the path. For numerical joint evaluation, the same marginal probabilities were clipped to $[10^{-8},1-10^{-8}]$ for the three principal fixed-margin models (independence, shared shock, and Gaussian factor-2). Complete-pattern log scoring additionally used the uniform probability mixing specified in Text S6. Marginal-model comparisons scored only old-cutoff-hidden analyte–location pairs, whereas joint evaluation used all six conditional margins. The endpoint-specific comparison used the fixed 20-point grid

\[
G_{20}=\{0,0.05,0.10,\ldots,0.95\}.
\]

For marginal score $s_{ij}(\alpha)$, the threshold summary was the arithmetic mean over the 20 grid values. Jurisdiction-error summaries used the same equal threshold weighting before aggregation across jurisdictions.

Five outcome-blind folds blocked by UCMR reporting jurisdiction were retained after strict filtering. Fold sizes were 5,305, 5,311, 5,287, 5,284, and 5,274. Fold 0 supported model development; folds 1–4 contained 21,156 locations used for the joint-model outer evaluation. Table S7 reports the all-five-fold final-release marginal diagnostics.

The architecture included a 67-position industrial-context branch derived from 2022 Toxics Release Inventory (TRI) records, EPA's PFAS-industry classification, and 2023 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Area representative points.<sup>6–8</sup> All 67 inputs were fixed at zero for every final-release prediction, so the reported models used only the site metadata and retained panel measurements defined above. The branch and its sigmoid gate were retained to preserve the architecture used during development. Earlier fits with observed industrial features informed model development but are not final-release evidence.

The continuous-threshold marginal model represented the six PFAS as analyte tokens in a shared censor-aware Transformer.<sup>9</sup> It used model dimension 64, two Transformer layers, four attention heads, a 128-unit feed-forward sublayer, dropout 0.1, a hidden native-reporting hurdle, and a truncated log-concentration head. Models were optimized with AdamW at learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 512, a maximum of 80 epochs, and early-stopping patience of 12 epochs. Random seeds were 20260810, 20260811, and 20260812. Categorical vocabularies were constructed within training folds, and Platt calibration used the designated validation fold only.<sup>10</sup> Frozen configuration files in the machine-readable supplement contain the remaining implementation details.

The comparator was a bank of 120 endpoint-specific models per fold: six PFAS by 20 thresholds. Across five folds, 592 of 600 endpoints used scikit-learn histogram gradient boosting classifiers;<sup>11</sup> eight endpoints without positive training examples used a smoothed-constant fallback. The two approaches shared folds, inputs with the TRI vector set to zero, validation-based calibration, analyte-specific scored rows, and a label-free cumulative-minimum projection. Paired intervals used 10,000 jurisdiction-cluster bootstrap replicates. Tables S7–S8 report the marginal-model results and paired threshold-averaged comparison. Full threshold curves remain in the machine-readable supplement.

The four input-block refits in Text S9 and Table S18 used the final archive and the same zero industrial-context inputs; they did not replace the principal marginal predictions reported in Table S7.
