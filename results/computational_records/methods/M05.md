## Text S5. Nested Evaluation and Fixed-Margin Joint Distributions

The marginal specification with the TRI vector set to zero and the two structured joint families were finalized after model diagnostics and failure of an earlier incompatible joint specification. The scored jurisdictions remained excluded from each fitted marginal and dependence model, but the family choice was informed by the broader analysis. The joint comparisons are exploratory post-selection predictive evaluations, not confirmatory estimates from an untouched holdout set.<sup>12</sup> The bootstrap intervals are conditional on these selected families.

For outer test fold $f\in\{1,2,3,4\}$, fold $v=1+(f\bmod4)$ was used for joint-model validation, and the other three folds supplied dependence-training observations. Fold 0 was not scored but could enter an outer-training pool. For every dependence-training target fold, the marginal model with the TRI vector set to zero was refitted while excluding both that target and the outer test fold. Dependence fitting paired these cross-fitted marginal probabilities with observed outcomes from the dependence-training folds. Outcomes from a dependence-training target fold entered the dependence loss but were excluded, together with outer-test outcomes, from the marginal model supplying probabilities for that fold.

All joint distributions were evaluated on the same 21,156 locations, 8,316 PWSs, 56 jurisdictions, and fixed query set

\[
Q=\{0.05,0.10,0.15,0.20,0.30,0.35,0.40,0.45,
0.55,0.60,0.65,0.70,0.80,0.85,0.90,0.95\},
\]

giving 338,496 location-threshold records per model. Dependence layers were fitted at $\alpha=0,0.5,1$ and evaluated at the 16 query values above.

The conditional-independence reference distribution was

\[
P_{\mathrm{fac}}(Y_i=y\mid p_i)
=\prod_{j=1}^{6}p_{ij}^{y_j}(1-p_{ij})^{1-y_j}.
\]

The conditional shared-shock layer extended a Marshall–Olkin construction.<sup>13</sup> For every nonempty subset $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,6\}$, let $T_S\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda_S)$ independently, with nonnegative rates summing to one. For analyte $j$, define

\[
Z_j=\min_{S:j\in S}T_S,
\qquad
r_j=\sum_{S:j\in S}\lambda_S,
\qquad
\tau_{ij}=-\frac{\log p_{ij}}{r_j}.
\]

Defining $Y_{ij}=I\{Z_j>\tau_{ij}\}$ gives $P(Y_{ij}=1)=p_{ij}$. For any subset $A$ of the six analytes, the joint survival moment is

\[
m_i(A)=\exp\left\{-\sum_{S:S\cap A\ne\emptyset}
\lambda_S\max_{j\in S\cap A}\tau_{ij}\right\},
\qquad m_i(\emptyset)=1.
\]

If $A_y=\{j:y_j=1\}$, inclusion–exclusion gives the 64-state probability

\[
q_i(y)=\sum_{B\subseteq A_y^c}(-1)^{|B|}m_i(A_y\cup B).
\]

The simplex constraint fixes the arbitrary time scale and leaves 62 nominal coordinates. Rates were fitted with a softmax parameterization and were used only as predictive dependence parameters.

The Gaussian comparator was a rank-2 multivariate probit factor model.<sup>14</sup> Let $F_i\sim N_2(0,I_2)$ and $\epsilon_{ij}\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}N(0,1)$, with independent factors and residuals. The model parameterized

\[
a_j=\frac{0.95b_j}{\sqrt{1+\lVert b_j\rVert^2}},
\]

which ensures $\lVert a_j\rVert<0.95$, and defined

\[
Z_{ij}=a_j^{\mathsf T}F_i+\sqrt{1-\lVert a_j\rVert^2}\,\epsilon_{ij},
\qquad
Y_{ij}=I\{Z_{ij}>\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{ij})\}.
\]

Conditional on $F_i=f$,

\[
\pi_{ij}(f)=\Phi\left\{
\frac{a_j^{\mathsf T}f-\Phi^{-1}(1-p_{ij})}
{\sqrt{1-\lVert a_j\rVert^2}}
\right\},
\]

and

\[
q_i(y)=\int
\prod_{j=1}^{6}\pi_{ij}(f)^{y_j}
[1-\pi_{ij}(f)]^{1-y_j}\,\phi_2(f)\,df.
\]

The model used 12-point two-dimensional Gauss–Hermite quadrature during fitting and 48-point quadrature for frozen evaluation. Sharing the latent Gaussian variables across thresholds produced a monotone coupling. The shared-shock family represents nonnegative common-shock dependence, whereas the Gaussian family restricts dependence to a two-factor correlation structure. Neither family is a general six-dimensional joint distribution, and neither its rates nor its factor loadings were interpreted as contamination sources or physical mechanisms.

A 17-component margin-preserving mixture served as a secondary positive-dependence diagnostic: an independent component, an all-six comonotone component, and 15 pairwise shared-uniform components. Each primary structured family used three multistarts per outer fold and a maximum budget of 180 epochs. Maximum objective spreads across multistarts were $5.93\times10^{-5}$ for the shared-shock fits and $8.58\times10^{-5}$ for the Gaussian fits. Selected solutions were near the training limit, so uniqueness of latent parameters was not claimed. Both structured families received the same cross-fitted marginal probabilities as the conditional-independence reference; their score differences therefore measure redistribution among the 64 reporting states conditional on those margins.
