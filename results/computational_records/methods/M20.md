## Text S20. Report Counts and Censored Targeted Concentration Sums

On the same supported first samples, the quantified sum Q was defined as the sum of concentrations reported at native limits, expressed in ng/L. It is an observed reporting summary. For unreported analytes, nonnegative concentrations were allowed anywhere below their native reporting limits, without assigning zero, one-half of the limit, or another imputed value. Consequently, Q is the lower endpoint and U = Q + sum of the unreported analytes' limits is a conservative upper endpoint for the concentration sum over the specified panel. Strict upper inequalities were represented by their limiting endpoint. These bounds do not account for analytical error in reported values. They concern the targeted analytes only, not unmeasured PFAS, fluorine mass balance, toxic equivalence, or health risk.

Source-specific mean counts, Q, and U used the original standardization weights. The source contrast for the full targeted sum was bounded by $Q_{SW}$ − $U_{GW}$ and $U_{SW}$ − $Q_{GW}$. Bootstrap intervals for each endpoint were computed from the same 1,999 estimable paired PWS-cluster draws as Text S19. The interval between identification endpoints is not a confidence interval; sampling intervals for the two endpoints are reported separately. The shared six, complete 25, PFBA/PFPeA/PFHxA trio, and remaining 16 added compounds partition the reporting summaries consistently (Table S37).

The surface-water-minus-groundwater difference in quantified sum was −0.363 ng/L for the shared six, 1.106 ng/L for all 25, and 1.449 ng/L for the added trio. Their 95% bootstrap intervals were −0.774 to 0.057, 0.441 to 1.797, and 1.126 to 1.806 ng/L. The corresponding full targeted-sum identification bounds were −20.352 to 19.687, −102.246 to 103.649, and −8.943 to 10.984 ng/L. Thus, a positive contrast in the quantified component did not establish a positive contrast after allowing all admissible unreported concentrations (Figure S6). The wide 25-analyte bounds reflect accumulation of censoring intervals, not evidence that concentrations approach their upper endpoints.

Descriptive ranking checks used location summaries and, separately, means across supported locations within each of 7,911 PWSs. Spearman correlation between the 25-analyte count and quantified sum was 0.999 over all locations but 0.894 among the 3,745 quantified-positive locations; corresponding PWS correlations were 0.997 and 0.916 among 2,107 positive systems. The large all-record correlations partly reflect shared ties at zero. Only 220,945 of 31,288,005 unordered PWS pairs had concentration-sum ordering resolved by nonoverlapping censoring intervals. In 3,920 pairs, the resolved concentration order opposed the strict mean-count order. These are descriptive pair counts, not independent observations or national system rankings. The complete rank and interval tables are supplied without selecting favorable cases.
