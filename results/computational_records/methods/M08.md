## Text S8. No-Retuning Evaluation across UCMR 5 Release Vintages

The final UCMR 5 archive downloaded on September 13, 2026 supplied all primary analyses. The January 15 archive defined only the frozen release-vintage evaluation. Strict six-PFAS events whose exact event keys were absent from the January archive and first appeared in the final archive formed the later-vintage score set. The January marginal models were reconstructed using their original reporting-jurisdiction folds, preprocessing, calibration procedures, threshold grids, configurations, and seeds; neither the later-vintage scoring records nor information introduced by the final release entered fitting, calibration, model selection, or hyperparameter tuning. The continuous-threshold model and the 120 endpoint-specific histogram-gradient-boosting models were evaluated on all 92 eligible events. Joint evaluation retained only the prespecified outer folds 1–4 and excluded development fold 0, leaving 69 events. The conditional-independence, shared-shock, and Gaussian-factor distributions received identical frozen marginal probabilities at 16 query thresholds.

First appearance in the archive did not necessarily indicate later sampling: 49 of the 92 events had collection dates on or before January 15, 2026, whereas 43 were collected later. The evaluation therefore tests transport across data releases, not temporal generalization to subsequently collected samples or pre-sampling prediction at new locations. The marginal comparison showed no clear advantage for the continuous-threshold model over endpoint-specific models (Table S16). In the formal joint set, the conditional-independence distribution nearly matched the pooled $K_6\ge2$ rate but misallocated probability between $K_6=0$ and $K_6=1$; the structured models improved complete-pattern and count scores while worsening the binary $K_6\ge2$ Brier score (Table S16). Only three sites contributed positive $K_6\ge2$ outcomes, so these comparisons are descriptive.

The no-retuning check evaluates the January models, not the subsequently refitted final-release models. Of the 69 joint-evaluation events, 41 belonged to administrative locations absent from the full January archive. Sixteen events came from seven PWSs absent from that archive; none contained two or more PFAS at the native reporting limits. Three events at preexisting locations had deleted six-analyte profiles with identical measurements, consistent with possible identifier revisions but not proof of the same physical water sample. Sequentially removing each of the 51 PWSs preserved the direction of the complete-pattern, count, and binary score differences in the full 69-event set. These event-equal influence checks do not enlarge the validation sample or resolve its sparse multi-PFAS outcomes.
