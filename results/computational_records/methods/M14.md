## Text S14. Conditional Prediction of Subsequent PFAS Identities

This exploratory diagnostic used the unchanged paired cohort from Text S13. It asked whether prior named reports supplied information about the composition of the next 25-analyte sample beyond its count, and whether a more flexible history mapping added value. Its protocol was fixed after inspecting the paired-observation results and before model fitting. It was not a new untouched confirmation set. The model family was a conditional Bernoulli distribution, an established construction rather than a new architecture.<sup>17</sup>

For a next-sample reported set S of size k and baseline features x, the conditional probability was proportional to exp of the sum of the analyte scores over S, normalized across all subsets of the 25 analytes having exactly k members. Equivalently, the normalizer was the degree-k elementary symmetric polynomial evaluated at the exponentiated 25 scores. Forward–backward dynamic programming evaluated this normalizer and its gradients exactly. Conditioning prevented gains from being attributed to predicting a different report count. The observed next count entered training and scoring as the condition defining the outcome distribution, never as a baseline feature. A complete forecast would additionally require a model for the future count.

Three prespecified models shared baseline source-water category, system-size category, sampling-month sine and cosine, count divided by 25, log of one plus count, and indicators for zero, one, or multiple reports. Each analyte had its own coefficients and intercept. The site-and-count model had 350 coefficients. The same-analyte memory model added one coefficient shared across analytes, multiplying each analyte's own baseline report indicator (351 coefficients total). The cross-analyte context model instead added a regularized 25-by-25 mapping from all baseline report indicators to next-sample analyte scores (975 coefficients total). The latter two models used the same measured information. Their contrast tests the specified forms of model complexity; the contrast against site and count also changes the information supplied. No later date, sampling interval, future measurement, or location identifier was a predictor.

The five pre-existing jurisdiction folds were retained. For outer test fold f, fold (f + 1) modulo 5 supplied inner validation and the other three folds supplied inner training. The L2 penalty was selected from 0.01 and 0.1 using mean conditional negative log-likelihood, then the model was refitted on all four non-test folds. Transformations were fitted using training data only. Deterministic L-BFGS fitting allowed 180 iterations, with an optimizer-only retry up to 400 iterations permitted by the protocol. All 30 candidate fits and 15 refits converged; all selected penalties were 0.01. Saved models, transformations, tuning scores, and fold assignments accompany the results.

The informative evaluation included 4,724 locations whose next counts were 1–24. At the other 21,032 locations, a count of zero or 25 determines the reported set and gives conditional log loss zero; these cases were not counted as evidence of identity skill. Primary scores weighted informative locations equally. PWS-equal and jurisdiction/PWS-equal scores are available in the machine-readable results. Paired intervals used the fixed-jurisdiction PWS bootstrap from Text S13 and condition on fitted scores; they exclude development and model-selection uncertainty. Subgroup scores by baseline count and by unchanged versus changed reported set are descriptive (Table S29). The latter groups use observed outcomes and are not available prospectively.

The site-and-count, same-analyte memory, and cross-analyte context losses were 2.910, 1.578, and 1.745, respectively (Table S29). The information contrast reduced loss by 1.332 (95% interval, 1.282–1.379), whereas the primary complexity contrast increased it by 0.167 (0.148–0.186; Table S29). The more complex model was worse in all five outer folds, and the direction was unchanged under the alternative weights. The reported 45.8% relative loss reduction is not a gain in classification accuracy. Prior identities were informative under this task, but the tested cross-analyte mapping did not improve on a single shared memory coefficient. Different parameter dimensions and the restricted penalty grid limit broader conclusions about model complexity; the search was not enlarged after observing these results.

Exhaustive enumeration, gradient checks, input-replacement tests, and independent score replay assessed computational consistency. Detailed tolerances and replay records are in the computational appendix. This count-conditioned diagnostic does not estimate contamination onset, continuous persistence, or a complete prospective prediction.
