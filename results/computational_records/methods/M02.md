## Text S2. Cross-Cycle Estimands, Threshold Decomposition, and Clustered Inference

For event $i$, analyte $j$, and analyte-specific threshold vector $L$, let $R_{ij}(L_j)=1$ when the archive contains a numeric result at or above $L_j$ and 0 otherwise. Thresholds were not evaluated below the applicable native MRL. The number of reported shared PFAS was

\[
K_{6i}(L)=\sum_{j=1}^{6}R_{ij}(L_j).
\]

The primary co-reporting endpoint was $K_6\ge2$. Additional endpoints, $K_6\ge r$, $r=1,\ldots,6$, described the complete reporting-count spectrum. This equally weighted count is not EPA's PFAS Hazard Index or a toxicity-weighted mixture metric.

Let $P_t(L)=P_t\{K_6(L)\ge2\}$ denote the probability of the primary endpoint in cycle $t$. The native-threshold contrast was decomposed exactly as

\[
P_5(L_5)-P_3(L_3)
=\{P_5(L_5)-P_5(L_3)\}
+\{P_5(L_3)-P_3(L_3)\}.
\]

The first component is the within-UCMR 5 reclassification produced by applying the older UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs. The second is the UCMR 5 minus UCMR 3 period contrast at the same UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs. The maximum numerical residual of this identity was $5.55\times10^{-17}$.

The primary estimand averaged events within PWS, PWSs within jurisdiction, and jurisdictions with equal weight. Event-equal and pooled matched-PWS-equal estimands were secondary. Confidence intervals used 20,000 bootstrap replicates under the resampling scheme corresponding to each estimand: jurisdiction-cluster bootstrap for the event-equal estimate, equal-PWS bootstrap for the pooled matched-PWS-equal estimate, and two-level jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap for the primary estimate and its matching or timing restrictions.<sup>5</sup> Table S3 reports estimates and intervals across the three estimands. Figure S1 compares the native-threshold contrast, within-UCMR 5 reclassification, and period contrast at UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs across estimands and restrictions. The archived count-spectrum data extend the primary analysis across the six-PFAS count spectrum. Each occurrence of a resampled jurisdiction received an independent within-jurisdiction PWS resample.

At the UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs in both cycles, the paired $2\times2$ table contained 7,635 locations negative in both cycles, six positive only in UCMR 5, 32 positive only in UCMR 3, and four positive in both (Table S13, Panel A). The discordant cells give an event-equal difference of −26/7,677, or −0.339 percentage points. An exact two-sided McNemar test gives $p=2.43\times10^{-5}$, but this test ignores PWS and jurisdiction clustering and is secondary to the weighted two-level bootstrap estimate.

Two deterministic perturbation calculations were performed (Table S13, Panel B). Within the fixed 7,677 pairs, converting 26 currently negative UCMR 5 statuses to positive would eliminate the unweighted common-cutoff difference; 27 would make it positive. In a separate conservative record-addition stress calculation, every hypothetical added native-cutoff-positive matched event was assumed to remain positive at the UCMR 3 cutoffs. With $x$ such events, the reclassification fraction is $819/(829+x)$; 82 and 195 events would be required to reduce it below 90% and 80%, respectively. These deterministic calculations quantify sensitivity of the small residual and the much larger reclassification fraction; they are not predictions of later administrative revisions. Panel C reports the prespecified cutoff perturbation from 1.00 to 2.00 times the UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs. The residual estimates remained small across this range.

The event indicator was aggregated to PWS and reporting-jurisdiction levels within the matched cohort. At least one $K_6\ge2$ event occurred in 26 PWSs under native UCMR 3 limits, 437 PWSs under native UCMR 5 limits, and 10 PWSs when UCMR 5 was evaluated at the UCMR 3 cutoffs. The corresponding numbers of reporting jurisdictions were 13, 34, and 8. These counts show how a reporting definition changes labels in the matched cohort; they are not prevalence estimates or evaluated operational decisions.

Sensitivity to the use of SE1 was assessed by independently reconstructing strict complete events for SE2, SE3, and SE4 with the same date- and sample-specific key. Locations were matched across cycles separately for each event code. Each event-specific cohort contains all eligible cross-cycle pairs for that code, without restriction to locations observed at every event position. Sample intervals were not standardized. The analysis tests sensitivity to sample-event position, not temporal invariance within a common longitudinal cohort. SE3 and SE4 contained surface-water, mixed-source, and groundwater-under-the-influence facilities but no groundwater facilities. Table S4 reports cohort composition, unweighted event counts, and the primary weighted contrasts.

The descriptive event-equal ratio $P(K_6\ge2\mid K_6\ge1)=n(K_6\ge2)/n(K_6\ge1)$ quantified multi-PFAS reporting among events with any reported PFAS. The ratios were 45.6% (36/79) for UCMR 3 at native MRLs, 61.3% (829/1,353) for UCMR 5 at native MRLs, and 17.2% (10/58) for UCMR 5 after applying the UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs. These ratios are separate from the primary weighted estimand. Their exact calculation and source hashes are supplied in `conditional_coreporting_literature_alignment.csv` and the machine-readable manifest.

Within the 829 UCMR 5 native-limit multi-PFAS events, applying the UCMR 3 numeric cutoffs produced 772 records with $K_6=0$, 47 with $K_6=1$, and 10 that retained $K_6\ge2$ (Table S17). At the PWS level, 1,984 of 2,421 matched systems had no multi-PFAS event under either UCMR 5 definition, 427 had one only at the native UCMR 5 limits, none gained the label only at the higher cutoffs, and 10 retained it under both definitions. Among the 829 native-limit multi-PFAS events, 61 distinct native-to-old-cutoff pattern transitions were observed. These deterministic transitions describe how alternative cutoffs reveal or conceal the same archived measurements.

The within-UCMR 5 reclassification was decomposed over all $6! = 720$ orders of replacing the six UCMR 5 MRLs with the corresponding UCMR 3 values. For each order, an analyte's marginal contribution was the change in $P(K_6\ge2)$ when its cutoff was replaced. Averaging over all orders gives an order-invariant Shapley decomposition. Under the primary jurisdiction/PWS estimand, the six contributions summed to 6.83463 percentage points, with a maximum additivity residual of $1.53\times10^{-16}$. The machine-readable Shapley table retains the analyte-specific estimates and intervals. These contributions quantify sensitivity to analyte-specific reporting-level changes, not sources or mechanisms.
