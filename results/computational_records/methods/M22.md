## Text S22. External Threshold Replication and Frozen-Model Evaluation

Washington data came from the Department of Health Sentry Internet water-quality exports, with sample-detail checks and system/source dictionaries.<sup>18</sup> The locked extraction spanned all 39 counties. Event keys, source-first selection, six-analyte completeness, finished-water status, qualifier handling, and raw UCMR system-overlap checks were applied before scoring. Explicit UCMR test panels were excluded. The retained 1,962 events belonged to 1,567 systems and covered 2021–2026. Reported numeric values remained distinct from less-than bounds; the latter were not replaced by zero concentrations. For the included Washington records, all six threshold labels were determined at or above the UCMR 5 cutoffs. Data hashes and all inclusion/exclusion records are in the accompanying audit.

The six lower cutoffs, in the order PFBS, PFHpA, PFHxS, PFNA, PFOA, and PFOS, were 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, and 4 ng/L; older cutoffs were 90, 10, 30, 20, 20, and 40 ng/L. These are imposed UCMR numeric thresholds, not two native Washington reporting regimes. Among the 81 lower-cutoff multi-PFAS events, older cutoffs produced 74 zero-report, four single-report, and three multi-report events (78/81 reclassified). These selected finished-water records do not estimate statewide prevalence.

West Virginia data used the USGS 2024 finished-water release, version 2.0 (September 2025), comprising 109 environmental samples at distinct sites.<sup>19</sup> The project targeted systems with earlier raw-water PFAS detections, rather than a representative statewide sample. Blanks, replicates, and laboratory controls were excluded from the environmental denominator. Retaining the published numerical estimates, all 14 lower-cutoff multi-PFAS samples became zero-report samples under the older cutoffs. One less-than result at 3.8 ng/L leaves the zero-versus-one classification uncertain but does not affect the multi-PFAS count. Estimated-value qualifiers affected 58 samples; excluding those entire samples left four lower-cutoff multi-PFAS samples among 51, all reclassified. The 14/14 result is conditional on the published numerical estimates. Treating estimated values as entirely unknown, rather than using their numerical estimates, yielded a 37.93–100% reclassification identification range, not a confidence interval. Missing historical model covariates and uncertified physical-system overlap precluded presenting this cohort as an independent predictive validation.

Washington prediction used the original fold-4 marginal ensemble, calibration, shared-shock model, and Gaussian factor-2 model. Washington jurisdictions were excluded from their fitting. Archived count priors and empirical-composition comparators were transported without re-estimation; their training folds 0, 2, and 3 contained 15,876 locations and no evaluation PWS. Historical score replay verified the archived parameters and priors. Overlap with raw UCMR system identifiers was excluded, but anonymous physical-site overlap with all earlier external research sources could not be certified. The external prediction protocol was locked after descriptive data inspection and before scoring. Subsequent decomposition and sensitivity analyses followed those scores and are exploratory; they do not convert model development into a prospectively untouched study.

Current system population and source metadata were available as proxies for 1,923 events from 1,542 systems. Eligibility required an unambiguous source match, valid population, an explicit groundwater or surface-water classification, and source activation preceding the sample without earlier inactivation. These conditions do not certify historical population or source mixtures. The other 39 events remained in eight all-event population/source-category scenarios. The proxy-subset contrasts, all-event scenario contrasts, and conservative eventwise category envelopes have distinct denominators and interpretations. The complete-pattern and count-log-score gain envelopes remained positive; additional gains beyond the count prior and conditional-identity gains did not share that robustness. Envelopes are not sampling intervals.

The fixed-margin evaluation in this section retained the original 16 internal threshold queries; its primary proxy subset contained 1,923 events. Scores were averaged over queries within event and then equally over events; 30,768 event–threshold records are not 30,768 independent samples. The original PWS bootstrap was supplemented with 10,000 paired draws under whole-PWS, whole-county, and nested county–PWS resampling, using seed 2026092604. Every PWS mapped to one county. Each selected county copy received an independent system resample; all source events and queries remained together. Ratios of resampled loss totals to event totals preserve the event-equal target, rather than averaging county means equally. Percentile intervals are unadjusted and conditional on frozen predictions and proxy inputs. They do not include fitting, selection, covariate, or all cross-county dependence uncertainty (Table S40).

Using the same joint distribution, complete-pattern log loss equals count log loss plus conditional-identity log loss. The archived count prior captured 96.8% and 96.0% of the shared-shock and Gaussian complete-pattern gains. Additional structured-model gains were unresolved under both county-level schemes. Outcome-defined count strata show where these gains arise (Table S41). The two-to-five-report stratum contains 291 event–threshold records from 66 events, 56 systems, and 19 counties. It has positive pattern and count gains, but negative conditional-identity point estimates with nested intervals spanning zero. Count-zero and count-six outcomes have only one possible identity and therefore zero conditional-identity loss. Conditional means use the stratum's support; contributions use all event–threshold records. Contributions over counts zero through six sum to the overall gain. The aggregate two-to-five row is not added again. Rare strata with zero-support bootstrap draws are explicitly flagged in the machine-readable table.

For numerical sensitivity, each normalized raw joint distribution was mixed with uniform mass at epsilon values of 10⁻⁸, 10⁻¹⁰, 10⁻¹², and 10⁻¹⁴. Count and identity scores were derived from that same mixture. All observed outcomes also had positive raw probability, permitting an unsmoothed check. Pattern gains over independence remained positive at every setting (Table S42). The largest pattern-gain change from the default 10⁻¹² was 0.001775; the unsmoothed change was only 0.000026. Identity point estimates remained negative. This scoring sensitivity is distinct from the frozen marginal interface's original near-one clipping; no prediction was changed to enforce exact boundary support.

External gains were geographically uneven: Spokane supplied about 45–47% of the net pattern-score gain, but excluding it left positive gains of 0.021981 and 0.023001. Removing any single county, laboratory, year, method panel, or system retained positive point estimates over independence. These influence checks do not imply positive gains in every subgroup; several county means were negative and the smaller 537-method panel remained uncertain. Most proxy events (1,915/1,923) had all six measurements masked at older cutoffs. Only 32 permitted input vectors remained, including 24 among fully masked events. The model could redistribute joint probability but could not distinguish locations sharing the same permitted inputs. At the lower endpoint, 78 multi-PFAS events were observed, versus 13.6 expected under independence, 50.0 under shared shock, and 45.6 under the Gaussian model. Better log scores therefore coexisted with underprediction of multi-PFAS event counts and uncertain conditional identity.
