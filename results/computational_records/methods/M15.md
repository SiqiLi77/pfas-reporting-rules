## Text S15. Panel-Dependent Source-Water Comparisons

The source-water analysis used 26,423 complete first-sample 25-analyte panels, without conditioning on subsequent sample availability. Groundwater and surface-water categories contained 25,544 locations; other categories contained 879. Common support required at least 20 locations and five PWSs of each source type within jurisdiction-by-system-size cells. The 59 eligible cells contained 20,162 locations in 34 jurisdictions; 5,382 groundwater/surface-water locations lay outside this support. The same eligible records were classified under six PFAS at older numeric cutoffs, six at native limits, and 25 at native limits. Cell weights were proportional to the combined number of eligible locations, fixed across source types and reporting schemes. Each source-specific rate was the weighted mean of its within-cell rates. These are standardized within-cohort comparisons, not national prevalence estimates or causal source-water effects.

The primary contrast was the change in the surface-water-minus-groundwater multi-report rate difference when the native-limit panel expanded from six to 25 analytes. Two thousand paired PWS-cluster bootstrap draws within fixed jurisdictions resampled all records of each selected system. Six draws had an empty source-specific denominator in at least one fixed support cell, leaving 1,994 finite draws for primary intervals. Alternative analyses gave PWSs equal weight within each source-cell, gave supported jurisdictions equal total weight, or removed one jurisdiction at a time. Panel-induced contrast changes were positive in all leave-one-jurisdiction-out point estimates. However, the absolute 25-panel source contrast changed sign after excluding Texas and under jurisdiction-equal weighting (Table S30; Figure S4). The interpretation is panel sensitivity, not a universal source-water ordering.

An explanatory 19-analyte Shapley decomposition held the shared six fixed and allocated each record's change across the two-report boundary to added analytes. If a record crossed from fewer than two to at least two reports, each of its m reported additional analytes received 1/m; otherwise all added-analyte contributions were zero. Averaging under the same source-specific standardization and subtracting groundwater from surface water produced analyte contributions to the panel-induced source contrast. Contributions summed exactly to the contrast change at the point estimate and in every finite bootstrap draw. Small-game exhaustive enumeration verified the expression. All 19 contributions, including zero and negative values, are retained in Table S31. Intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous. This classification-based decomposition does not quantify chemical mass, toxicity, or source contributions.

A post-result timing sensitivity added collection year or year-quarter to jurisdiction-size cells. Each finer cell required at least 10 locations and three PWSs of each source type. The original coarse standardization was recalculated on exactly the same timing-supported records, separating altered support from added adjustment. Year support retained 18,145 locations; year-quarter support retained 11,665. Timing-adjusted panel contrasts were 5.068 and 6.352 percentage points, versus 4.883 and 6.401 under coarser weighting of the respective same-record cohorts (Table S34). Fine-cell resampling left 1,844 and 1,636 finite draws of 2,000 because some source denominators became empty. These sparse-cell checks are therefore reported as descriptive point estimates, not as improved precision or independent confirmation. The full bootstrap draws and finite-draw counts are archived. The timing protocol was fixed after the original source results, before these additional estimates were calculated.
