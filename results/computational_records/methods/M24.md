## Text S24. Additional-Information and Training-Strategy Sensitivities

### Scope and evaluation

These post-result sensitivities retained the 26,423 national events, five jurisdiction-blocked partitions, three training-selected auxiliaries, and permitted-input rules in Text S23. Candidate sets and selection rules were fixed before their respective fits, following earlier result inspection. Washington was excluded from fitting and tuning; its primary subset contained 1,819 events in 1,456 PWSs. Eight covariate scenarios reused 1,854 eligible events. National scores were jurisdiction/PWS-equal; Washington scores were PWS-equal. Paired intervals used 5,000 PWS-cluster draws, with jurisdiction/county sensitivity, conditional on saved predictions rather than full development uncertainty. These previously examined cohorts support sensitivity analysis; none of the added candidates replaced the main-figure models.

### Information and conditional identity

Without refitting, auxiliary count alone reduced Washington nonlinear pattern loss by 17.9% and binary Brier loss by 32.1% relative to the no-auxiliary model. Additional identities and concentrations had unresolved pattern-score benefits (Table S49); this is not an equivalence result. Full auxiliary inputs retained gains among 1,812 all-hidden-target events (18.2% and 33.1%), and the pattern advantage survived deletion of each county, laboratory, year, PWS, or event.

Identity controls shared the nonlinear predicted count distribution but could change PFAS margins. Predicted distributions for all seven counts were combined; observed counts entered training and scoring only. Hierarchical frequencies conditioned successively on old-visible reports, regional/system attributes, auxiliary count, identities, and concentration bins. Five shrinkage choices included parent fallback. Shared-effect alternatives combined an empirical pattern prior, six analyte effects, and 15 pair effects; controls omitted or fixed pair effects, used auxiliary count alone, or changed weights. Three finite penalties and complete shrinkage were selected on validation jurisdictions.

Hierarchical identity gains were unresolved in both primary cohorts. Relative to training empirical identity, the shared-effect model’s national conditional-identity gain had a jurisdiction-resampling interval crossing zero and was not resolved in Washington (Table S50). Two PWSs contributed 91.3% of that national net gain; under record-equal weighting, the relative gain was 0.73%, with an interval crossing zero. No influential record was removed. Among 3,491 national and 133 Washington informative events, 13 and one had unseen training patterns, but 40.2% and 45.9% lacked support in the finest context-by-count cells. This describes model-specific sparsity, not a general recovery limit.

### Calibration and threshold-path sensitivities

Count calibration, convex mixing of four saved distributions, and mixing followed by calibration used geographic validation subfolds and allowed the unchanged model. Mixing reduced national pattern loss by 1.17%; Washington's point estimate was unfavorable and its interval crossed zero. Mixing plus calibration had no resolved overall advantage.

Coherent-path alternatives fixed the native joint distribution and learned five concentration-interval probabilities. Higher cutoffs could only remove reports. Predictions integrated all 64 possible native patterns; neither the observed pattern nor hidden concentrations were inputs. Interval interiors used uniform normalized log concentration. Transitions were pattern-independent or conditioned on latent native count/full pattern, with linear, nonlinear, fixed-frequency, and two-component controls. Native predictions remained unchanged; retained inputs determined old-cutoff outcomes.

Scores averaged four interior thresholds (0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.95) within event before weighting; five off-grid thresholds checked interpolation. Pattern-conditioned transitions improved national pattern loss by 2.19% relative to independently fitted nonlinear endpoints, with positive PWS and jurisdiction intervals, but not a resolved Washington gain (Table S50). Coherent candidates satisfied marginal and count-tail monotonicity. These auxiliary paths do not replace the original continuous-threshold marginal model.

Four-threshold fine-tuning used parameter anchoring and unchanged-model fallback. A matched variant used geographically cross-fitted base probabilities in training and the original base model in validation/deployment. Neither reduced national primary pattern loss relative to the unchanged pattern-conditioned path; Washington selected the unchanged-path fallback. Detailed development specifications and verification records are retained in the computational appendix.
