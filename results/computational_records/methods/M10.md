## Text S10. Same Sample Panel Coverage and Concentration Reassignment

### Expanded panel eligibility and reporting composition

The expanded analysis used the 25 analytes assigned to EPA Method 533 in the final UCMR 5 occurrence archive. Each retained location had exactly one SE1 result per analyte, one collection date, one SampleID, consistent metadata, and no competing result among those analytes. Eligibility was reconstructed directly from the raw archive rather than from historical 29-analyte pretraining tables. Those earlier tables were not designed to establish same-sample coverage. Combining EPA Methods 533 and 537.1 without checking collection dates and sample identifiers was not permitted.

The strict 25-analyte panel covered 7,662 of 7,677 matched locations (99.80%), 2,419 PWSs, and 57 jurisdictions. Of 15 excluded matched events, 14 had no native six-PFAS report and one had a single report; all 829 native six-PFAS multi-report events and the 10 multi-report events at old cutoffs remained eligible. A secondary analysis covered 26,423 of the 26,461 strict six-PFAS locations (99.86%). The larger-panel denominators therefore differ from the matched and predictive cohorts elsewhere in this study. The shared-six and 25-analyte native summaries use the same reporting limit for each shared analyte. No old-cycle cutoff was assigned to an additional analyte.

The three descriptive reporting definitions were the six shared PFAS at old numeric cutoffs, the same six at native UCMR 5 limits, and the 25 PFAS at their native limits. Event-level count transitions were summarized before aggregation to PWS. In the matched expanded-panel cohort, the number of systems with at least one multi-report event was 10, 437, and 621, respectively, among 2,419 PWSs. These counts are conditional on panel eligibility and are not national prevalence estimates. The full chemical lists, native reporting limits, selection exclusions, and exact observed patterns are in the machine-readable supplement.

Event-equal proportions were primary for panel coverage. PWS-equal and jurisdiction/PWS-equal point estimates were recalculated within the eligible cohort rather than reusing weights from all 7,677 matched events. Each bootstrap replicate independently sampled PWS clusters with replacement within every fixed jurisdiction stratum, retaining all locations of a selected system. There were 10,000 replicates with seed 20260924. Conditional proportions were recomputed from resampled numerators and denominators. These intervals quantify within-cohort cluster resampling variation and are distinct from the two-level jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap for the main cross-cycle and model comparisons. Table S22 reports coverage and the key conditional proportions; Figure 2c uses observed event-equal fractions without uncertainty bars.

The observed counts increased from 10 to 829 to 1,180 multi-report events across the three definitions, but the increase under panel expansion is mechanically constrained by set inclusion. The informative result is the redistribution of zero and singleton six-panel records within the same samples. In the 25-panel-positive subset, 359 of 1,711 events had none of the six shared PFAS reported. In the 25-panel-multiple subset, 159 had no shared-six report and 192 had one, totaling 351 of 1,180 events. Exact 25-panel patterns in the shared-six-zero group included PFHxA+PFPeA in 74 events and PFBA+PFHxA+PFPeA in 43. Among the 772 native six-panel multi-report events reclassified as zero at old cutoffs, 551 also reported PFAS outside the shared six. Retaining all 772 as multiple in the wider native panel follows from their definition and is not treated as additional evidence.

### Concentration reassignment reference

A separate exploratory reference analysis examined the arrangement of reported concentrations across the observed native patterns. The cohort remained the full 7,677 matched locations. Native reporting masks were fixed, and concentrations of each reported analyte were independently reassigned among native-positive events within jurisdiction-by-source-water strata. Source-water-only and unrestricted reassignment were sensitivity references. Singleton strata were unchanged. The operation preserved the complete unweighted concentration distribution for each analyte, hence its retention count at every queried cutoff, along with every event's native reporting pattern. Nondetect concentrations remained unknown and were not imputed as zero. The code permuted each observed concentration's equivalent last-passing threshold index, which gives exactly the same reporting outcomes on the fixed grid.

Twenty-one logarithmically interpolated cutoff positions, including both endpoints, were used. Fractions becoming zero-report or retaining multiple reports were computed within the original 829 native multi-report events. The primary statistic was the trapezoidal integral of the latter fraction over the fixed alpha grid; the zero-report integral was secondary. There were 2,000 seeded reassignments per reference, with the count fixed before scoring. Randomization quantiles describe these references rather than population confidence intervals. A maximum standardized deviation over both curves at interior cutoff positions defined a 95% simultaneous reference envelope; endpoints were excluded from that envelope (Figure S2). Full pattern tables and sensitivity references were retained.

Under jurisdiction-by-source-water reassignment, the observed event-equal retained-multiple integral was 0.271984 versus reference mean 0.257344, and the zero-report integral was 0.543185 versus 0.501407. Both exceeded their respective 95% reference ranges (Table S24). The two increases indicate fewer singleton outcomes integrated over this grid, not simply uniformly faster loss. Under jurisdiction/PWS weighting, both observed integrals lay within the corresponding reference ranges, although the interior-path maximum-deviation statistic still departed from the stratified reference (reference-tail probability 0.00050). Integrated and localized path discrepancies therefore need not agree. The weighted margins were not fixed by event reassignment, so these results cannot establish additional dependence at fixed weighted margins.

The reference also breaks associations between an analyte's concentration and the original native report pattern. It preserves analyte retention in the full matched cohort, not within the 829-event multi-report subset. The result is therefore not a pure test of cross-analyte concentration dependence conditional on that subset's margins. Concentration exchangeability within strata is an analytical assumption, and PWS-level concentration clustering is not preserved. These checks are descriptive support for the observed reporting transformations, not causal evidence for shared sources or chemical mechanisms.
