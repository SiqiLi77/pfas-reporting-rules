## Text S12. Restricted Baseline Information and Subsequent Wider Panel Reports

This exploratory analysis asked whether limited baseline measurements retained information about later multi-PFAS reports outside the historical shared-six summary. Among 26,461 strict SE1 locations, 24,456 initially reported no more than one shared PFAS at native reporting levels. The first date strictly later than SE1 was selected from SE2–SE4 records for the fixed Method 533 analyte set before checking its completeness or report status. A valid outcome required exactly one record for each of the 25 analytes, one SampleID, one sample-event code, and valid methods, units, and reporting levels. An incomplete earliest date was not replaced by a later complete sample. Exclusion of 498 locations without a later date and 77 with an invalid or incomplete first later panel left 23,881 locations in 9,691 PWSs and 66 jurisdictions. The median interval was 179 days, with a range of 1–1,211 days; this was not a fixed six-month prediction horizon. The target required at least two of the 25 PFAS reported in the subsequent sample and at most one of the shared six reported in that same sample. There were 917 targets, including 595 at locations initially reporting none of the six. All subsequent 25-panel multi-PFAS events (n = 1,374) formed a secondary outcome evaluated with the same primary-target rankings.

The site input block contained initial water type, system size, region, and sampling-month sine and cosine. The six-analyte block contained baseline report flags and log concentration/MRL for reported measurements, together with fixed positive-part hinges at log(2), log(5), and log(10). Unreported values received a constant feature code and an explicit report flag, not a concentration imputation. State defined folds and allocation strata only. Location identifiers, subsequent measurements, follow-up dates and intervals, and the additional 19 baseline analytes were excluded from model inputs. Replacement of outcome fields, additional-analyte fields, and hidden concentration values left the permitted feature matrices and tree predictions unchanged.

Five pre-existing jurisdiction folds separated training, validation, and evaluation. For evaluation fold f, validation used (f + 1) modulo 5 and training used the other three folds. PWSs never crossed these partitions. Category vocabularies and transformations were fitted on training data. L2-penalized logistic regression selected its penalty from 0.0001, 0.001, 0.01, and 0.1 using validation log loss. The empirical comparator estimated training outcome frequency within shared-six report pattern, water type, and system size, using 20 pseudo-observations at the smoothed training prevalence; unseen cells received that prevalence. Additional references were random allocation, singleton-first ranking, and reported concentration. With zero or one report, maximum and summed reported concentration were identical.

A second frozen round compared XGBoost 3.0.5 with the unchanged regression and empirical predictions.<sup>16</sup> Site-only, six-only, and combined trees used the same permitted features. Six configurations crossed maximum depths of 2, 3, and 4 with minimum child weights of 5 and 20. Histogram trees used a learning rate of 0.03, L2 leaf penalty of 10, full row and feature sampling, and at most 1,000 rounds. Early stopping used validation log loss with patience 80. The configuration with lowest validation log loss was retained; evaluation outcomes did not select hyperparameters. There was no class weighting, synthetic oversampling, or test-based calibration. Main blocked fits were not refitted on validation data. Tuning results from all 108 candidate fits and the 18 retained model specifications are archived; three calendar models were additionally refitted on permissible past records.

The primary allocation selected 20% of eligible locations within each jurisdiction. Exact fractional selection at score ties and budget boundaries represented expected randomized allocation rather than an implemented sampling program. Target recall was the fraction of the 917 targets selected; yield was targets per selected location. Budget curves from 5% to 100% were descriptive. Paired 95% intervals used 2,000 PWS bootstrap draws within fixed jurisdictions, with budgets reallocated in each draw and fitted scores held fixed. They do not include full model-selection uncertainty or multiplicity adjustment. The second round's primary contrast was combined trees minus empirical-frequency recall at 20%. Allocations separately reranked within initially zero-report locations answer a different question from attribution of the single full-cohort allocation. Tables S25 and S26 retain both views and secondary probability scores.

The temporal sensitivity trained on outcomes collected by December 31, 2024 and evaluated baseline samples collected from January 1, 2025, excluding evaluation PWSs from training. It retained 15,344 earlier training pairs and 5,051 evaluation pairs with 174 targets. Fold 0 of the earlier data selected penalties and tree counts; the selected specifications were then refitted on all permissible earlier pairs. Target recall at 20% was 53.9% for empirical frequency, 58.4% for combined regression, and 52.5% for combined trees. Although calendar tree average precision exceeded the regression value, allocation recall did not improve (Tables S25–S26). Neither metric alone establishes general model superiority. This internally separated check uses previously examined archive data and is not independent confirmation.

The richer-information reference ranked locations by their observed initial 25-analyte reporting count. All comparisons were reranked on the same 23,846 locations with complete baseline panels and 912 targets. Its 76.0% recall, versus 48.5% for combined regression and 43.2% for combined trees, concerns additional measurements rather than an equal-information algorithm comparison. Of these 912 targets, 527 already had multiple initial 25-panel reports, 168 had one, and 217 had none. Another five targets lacked a complete initial panel and were excluded from this common-subset comparison. Under the full-cohort regression allocation, 97.4% of missed targets arose at initially six-negative locations. The observed composition frequently included PFPeA, PFHxA, and PFBA, without identifying a source or health consequence.

A post-fit descriptive audit attributed each common-subset allocation to the initial 25-panel reporting state (Table S25, Panel D). Selection probabilities, the 20% within-jurisdiction budget, outcomes, and fitted scores were unchanged; no reranking within reporting-state groups or new model fitting was performed. With fractional selection at ties, expected captured targets summed to 692.68 for the count reference and 442.05 for regression. Their net difference of 250.63 comprised 199.68 among initially multiple-report targets, 80.08 among initially single-report targets, and -29.13 among initially zero-report targets. The first contribution was 79.7% of the net difference, a descriptive ratio rather than a causal decomposition or an inferential estimate. The count reference selected 5.3% of the 217 targets initially reporting none of the 25 PFAS, compared with 18.7% for regression. These percentages attribute the common full-cohort allocation; they do not describe separate 20% budgets within each reporting-state group. Reports at two sampling dates do not establish onset or continuous persistence. Neither this audit nor failure of the tested nonlinear learner establishes an upper bound on prediction or validates reduced monitoring.
