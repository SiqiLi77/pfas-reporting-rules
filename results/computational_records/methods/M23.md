## Text S23. Auxiliary Measurements, Information Content, and Frozen Geographic Transport

### Cohort, selection, and information sets

This exploratory extension used 26,423 first-event locations with 25 distinct EPA Method 533 analytes in the same sample, from 10,236 PWSs and 66 reporting jurisdictions. Requiring the full panel excluded 38 of the 26,461 strict six-analyte events. The six targets remained PFBS, PFHpA, PFHxS, PFNA, PFOA, and PFOS. All other 19 measured PFAS were eligible auxiliaries. Target concentrations below UCMR 3 cutoffs never entered the predictors. The baseline contained water type, system-size category, EPA region, collection-month sine/cosine, and the six older-cutoff-visible flags and log(1 + concentration/older cutoff) values. Location, PWS, and jurisdiction identifiers were excluded. Each auxiliary contributed a native-limit report flag and log(concentration/native limit) when reported. A zero-filled log feature was accompanied by the flag and did not impute a zero concentration.

The original five jurisdiction-blocked folds were retained. For evaluation fold f, validation used (f + 1) modulo 5 and training used the remaining three folds. Encoders, transformations, and analyte selection used training data only. Ranking used conditional mutual information between a three-level auxiliary measurement and the target pattern given its count. Levels were unreported, [MRL, 2 MRL), and at least 2 MRL; eligibility required at least 20 training reports. Ties were broken by analyte name. Each fold selected PFHxA, PFPeA, and PFBA, although their order varied. These are perfluorohexanoic, perfluoropentanoic, and perfluorobutanoic acids, with imposed lower cutoffs of 3, 3, and 5 ng/L. Their appearance in the earlier descriptive decomposition did not replace training-only selection.

The initial protocol compared no auxiliaries, one/two/three selected auxiliaries, all 19, three random nested orders at budgets one/two/three, and a shuffled-three control. Shuffling was performed separately in each partition within region × water-type × size × old-visible-mask strata. It preserved auxiliary relationships and coarse context but changed only 14–28% of event vectors where strata permitted exchanges. This is a single negative control, not a permutation test. A separately locked follow-up compared no auxiliary information, the auxiliary report count encoded as four indicator categories, the three individual report flags, and flags with reported concentrations. The auxiliary count is not $K_6$. These input comparisons do not hold the six predicted marginal probabilities fixed.

### Joint probabilities and inference without the true count

Six penalized logistic regressions supplied an independent reference. Linear and nonlinear models instead fitted separate count and conditional-identity heads. The nonlinear head used one 64-unit tanh layer. For every compatible six-bit pattern y, predictions were formed as

\[
q_i(y\mid x_i)=q_i(K_6=|y|\mid x_i)\,
q_i(y\mid K_6=|y|,x_i).
\]

All seven count probabilities and every within-count pattern probability were generated from the permitted inputs. The observed target count selected the loss term during training and scoring only; it was not provided at inference. Patterns contradicting an older-cutoff-visible target were masked before normalization. The models estimated reports at UCMR 5 numeric cutoffs rather than latent occurrence above zero. Training selected penalties from 0.0001 and 0.01 using validation loss separately for the two heads. Linear fits used deterministic L-BFGS with a 500-iteration cap. Nonlinear fits used Adam, learning rate 0.01, at most 500 epochs, validation every five epochs, and 60-epoch patience. No refitting followed selection.

Conditional-identity log loss was the initial protocol's primary diagnostic, reported both over all events and conditional on $1\le K_6\le5$. Complete-pattern, count, binary, and marginal scores were secondary. Counts zero and six admit one identity and contribute zero identity loss. Pattern, count, and all-event identity losses were derived from one distribution with uniform mixing mass $10^{-12}$, preserving the exact log-score decomposition. Conditioning identity loss on counts one to five changes its denominator; it cannot be subtracted directly from all-event pattern loss. Tables S44–S45 distinguish the two identity summaries. National estimates give jurisdictions equal weight and PWSs equal weight within jurisdiction. Paired PWS bootstrap within fixed jurisdictions and jurisdiction-resampling sensitivity used 5,000 draws; event-equal estimates are retained in the computational supplement. Intervals condition on saved predictions and do not incorporate refitting or prior development selection.

### External auxiliary eligibility and frozen prediction

The source-first Washington cohort was fixed before this extension (Text S22). Required auxiliary records shared the exact system, source, collection date, laboratory, and sample identifier. Missing assays were not coded as unreported. Numeric results below the imposed cutoff and less-than bounds at or below it were classified as unreported; a less-than bound above the cutoff would be unresolved. Units had to be ng/L, with an unqualified routine/compliance finished-water measurement. Of 1,962 locked events, 1,854 had all three usable auxiliary measurements, from 1,481 PWSs across 39 counties. Exclusions were 107 events missing PFPeA and PFBA and one missing PFPeA alone; none were replaced by later samples. The current-metadata proxy subset comprised 1,819 events and 1,456 PWSs, including 72 multi-target events and 133 events with target counts one to five (Table S43).

The baseline and selected-three checkpoints from fold 4 excluded Washington from both fitting and validation. All three model classes were applied without refitting, recalibration, auxiliary reselection, or selection by external scores. Eight hypothetical water-type/size scenarios crossed GW, SW, GU, and MX categories with small/large system size for all 1,854 events. The primary proxy subset retained the earlier metadata eligibility rule. Its estimand was PWS-equal at the native UCMR 5 numeric endpoint. This differs from Text S22, which averaged 16 thresholds within event and used event-equal scores on 1,923 events. The two percentage improvements must not be compared as if they used the same records, reference model, or scoring target.

Paired PWS-cluster and whole-county resampling used 5,000 draws. County draws retained PWS-equal weighting through the ratio of sampled PWS loss totals to sampled PWS counts; they did not redefine the point estimate as county-equal. Full-pattern and binary improvements remained positive under both schemes and all eight scenarios (Table S46). The nonlinear proxy subset's identity improvement conditional on counts one to five had a PWS interval spanning zero and a positive county interval. Neither interval was selected as definitive. Washington outcomes had been inspected before the extension, so this is frozen geographic transport on previously examined data, not prospectively untouched validation. Historical covariates and all anonymous physical-site overlaps remain uncertified.

### Threshold, training, and simpler-model checks

Four independently fitted interior endpoints used the original geometric threshold path at alpha = 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, and 0.95. The native endpoint reused the initial models; at alpha = 1 the older-cutoff-visible inputs determined all target labels without fitting. Inputs remained fixed while target cutoffs changed. Absolute pattern-score gains declined toward this deterministic boundary. Marginal reporting probabilities increased across some adjacent thresholds in both model families, with maximum increases of approximately 0.5 probability units. These models consequently do not define a coherent continuous-threshold process and do not replace Text S4. All violations, positive and negative endpoint effects, and label counts are reported in Table S47 and the source tables. Declining gains cannot be interpreted as weaker environmental dependence at higher concentrations (Tables S45 and S47).

Two additional fixed seed offsets, 10,000 and 20,000, retained the training rules. Nonlinear native-endpoint pattern-loss reductions were 21.62%, 20.09%, and 21.58% across the original and additional seeds; conditional-identity intervals under jurisdiction resampling spanned zero for all three. The selected-three count heads in folds 0 and 3 were also extended from a 500- to 1,500-epoch cap, keeping the original identity heads. Validation selected epochs 1,315 and 670. Pooled pattern loss increased by 0.000011, with an interval spanning zero, so the original model was retained. Calibration was descriptive, using fixed probability bins [0, .01, .025, .05, .1, .2, .4, .6, .8, 1], without post-hoc recalibration. For the national nonlinear model with three auxiliary measurements, the jurisdiction/PWS-weighted mean probability of $K_6\ge2$ was 6.66%, versus 5.65% observed, despite improved Brier score. A closer external mean does not establish calibration across probability ranges.

A post-result protocol added empirical-identity and conditional-Bernoulli comparators (Table S48). The empirical-identity comparator added a 0.5 pseudocount to each training-fold pattern frequency, restricted and renormalized frequencies within each count to patterns compatible with old-visible reports, and combined them with the frozen nonlinear count probabilities for the corresponding input condition. The latter assigns within-count probability proportional to exp(sum of the reported-analyte logits), using linear predictors, and combines it with the same nonlinear count distribution. Auxiliary measurements improved the conditional-Bernoulli identity score, but the selected-three nonlinear identity model had no resolved advantage over that comparator. Thus a favorable pattern or binary result cannot substitute for the primary identity diagnostic or establish neural-architecture superiority. Model budgets, random selections, shuffled controls, and all less favorable comparisons remain available in the computational supplement.

Input-replacement, fold-separation, independent prediction, and scoring-replay checks assessed computational consistency. Protocol timing, numerical tolerances, unit-test counts, and replay scope are consolidated in the computational appendix; these checks do not establish untouched validation or clean retraining.
