## Text S6. Predictive Scores, Calibration, and Influence Analyses

The primary predictive endpoint was negative log-likelihood (NLL) for the observed 64-state reporting pattern.<sup>15</sup> To avoid eventwise clipping, probabilities were mixed with a uniform distribution:

\[
\widetilde P=(1-10^{-12})P+10^{-12}/64.
\]

Secondary endpoints were the continuous ranked probability score and Brier score for the seven-category count distribution, together with Brier score and average precision for $K_6\ge2$. For location $i$, each joint score was averaged equally across the 16 fixed queries,

\[
\bar\ell_i=\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{\alpha\in Q}\ell_i(\alpha),
\qquad |Q|=16.
\]

This discrete average is not an integral over the threshold path. The primary score estimand averaged within PWS, within jurisdiction, and equally across jurisdictions. Intervals used 20,000 jurisdiction–PWS two-level bootstrap replicates of the frozen outer-test loss differences. Neither marginal nor dependence models were refitted in the bootstrap. The intervals quantify clustered variation conditional on the frozen predictions and model families; they exclude uncertainty from refitting or family choice. Each occurrence of a resampled jurisdiction received an independent within-jurisdiction PWS resample.

Figure S7 shows pooled PWS-equal complete-pattern NLL gains relative to conditional independence at the 16 prespecified cutoff positions. Gains contracted toward the UCMR 3 cutoffs and became slightly negative at threshold positions 0.85–0.95 for the shared-shock model and 0.80–0.95 for the Gaussian model; the simultaneous intervals at these positions included zero. As query cutoffs approach the older limits, the target reporting pattern approaches the old-visible mask. The input information remains fixed along the path. The path characterizes predictive performance in the reporting task and does not establish weaker environmental dependence at higher concentrations. Simultaneous intervals resampled PWSs as a single level; they did not use the primary two-level jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap.

The archived reliability-bin data compare pooled record-equal reliability for the conditional-independence reference and the conditional shared-shock model using 10 model-specific equal-count bins across 338,496 records. Table S11 reports binary calibration and ranking for both structured families and the 17-component diagnostic. This pooled reliability analysis is secondary to the jurisdiction/PWS-weighted proper-score comparisons and does not imply improvement in binary Brier score. 

Numerical checks verified supplied-margin preservation, state normalization, nonincreasing marginal probabilities, and score reconstruction. Adjacent-threshold Boolean-lattice transport checks found no shared-shock violation at the specified tolerance. Residuals and numerical tolerances are retained in the computational verification record and validation manifest.

In the pooled record-equal decomposition, records with $K_6\ge2$ comprised 1.9658% of event–threshold records and contributed 90.91% of the net shared-shock NLL gain. This contribution analysis uses different weights from the primary score comparison. Under the primary jurisdiction/PWS weights, the all-unreported pattern contributed positively, several multi-PFAS patterns gained score, and common single-PFAS patterns lost score (Table S14). These contributions combine count and identity information; main-text Figure 4b and Table S19 separate those components. Pattern-NLL gains remained positive in each outer fold, every leave-one-jurisdiction and leave-one-PWS recalculation, and analyses removing up to 10% of PWSs with the largest positive contributions (Table S12). Full pattern, count-stratum, and fold results remain indexed in the computational supplement.
