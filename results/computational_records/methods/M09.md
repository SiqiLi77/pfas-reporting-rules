## Text S9. Supplementary Evaluation of Information Sources and Joint Prediction

### S9.1. Information-block comparisons

Marginal-model training was repeated under four input conditions: both blocks, site metadata alone, visible co-analytes alone, and neither block. Site metadata comprised sampling month, region, PWS size, and water-source category. The co-analyte block comprised old-cutoff reporting indicators, visible concentration values, and the number of visible analytes. Input removal occurred after preprocessing and did not alter the target labels or old-cutoff-hidden risk sets. The neither-block condition retained analyte identity, reporting cutoffs, training-derived parameters, and the common risk-set definition. All 67 industrial-context inputs remained zero. The architecture, five jurisdiction folds, three seeds per fold, calibration, and early stopping were unchanged, giving 60 fits. For test fold $f$, validation used fold $(f+1)\bmod5$, and the remaining three folds supplied training observations. These explanatory fits did not replace the principal marginal model or refit its joint dependence layers.

The main input comparison used folds 1–4 and the original 20 threshold positions. Squared probability errors were averaged across thresholds, then across at-risk locations within each analyte, and finally equally across the six PFAS. This event-equal marginal score differs from the primary jurisdiction/PWS-equal joint score. A secondary marginal summary used equal PWS weights within jurisdiction and equal jurisdiction weights before averaging over analytes. All five specified input contrasts are reported in Table S18; pointwise intervals used 20,000 paired jurisdiction-cluster bootstrap replicates conditional on fitted predictions. These post-development comparisons are explanatory, not independent confirmation.

For visibility group $g$, let $R_j$ be the full scored risk set for analyte $j$ and $N_j$ its size. The descriptive contribution was

\[
D_g=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{j=1}^{6}\frac{1}{N_j}
\sum_{i\in R_j\cap g}\frac{1}{20}\sum_{a=1}^{20}
\left[(y_{ija}-p^{\mathrm{site}}_{ija})^2-(y_{ija}-p^{\mathrm{both}}_{ija})^2\right].
\]

The groups with at least one old-cutoff-visible PFAS and with none visible partition each risk set; their contributions therefore sum to the overall marginal Brier reduction. They are not subgroup means multiplied by the overall location fraction. This decomposition identifies where the fitted score difference occurred and does not attribute the joint-model gain to an input block.

### S9.2. Separating reported counts from constituent identities

For a uniformly stabilized 64-state distribution $\widetilde q$, define the count probability and conditional identity probability by

\[
q_K(k)=\sum_{s:|s|=k}\widetilde q(s),
\qquad r(s\mid k)=\frac{\widetilde q(s)}{q_K(k)}.
\]

For the observed reporting state $y$ and $k=|y|$, the log score decomposes exactly:

\[
-\log\widetilde q(y)=-\log q_K(k)-\log r(y\mid k).
\]

All three terms were computed from the same stabilized distribution, without separately flooring the count or conditional probabilities. At counts zero and six, only one reporting state is possible and conditional-identity loss is zero. Paired reductions relative to conditional independence were aggregated with the primary weights and evaluated using 20,000 independently nested jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap replicates (Table S19). The count share is the count-score reduction divided by the net pattern-score reduction. It can exceed 100% when the identity component is negative; it is not a causal proportion.

The conditional identity distribution was also evaluated with the proper score

\[
B_{\mathrm{id}}(y)=\sum_{s:|s|=k}
\left[r(s\mid k)-I(s=y)\right]^2.
\]

This score assesses which PFAS were reported conditional on the observed number and is not a strictly proper score for the full joint distribution. Its paired intervals used the same primary weighting and independently nested resampling (Table S21). Lack of a resolved improvement in either identity score is not an equivalence result.

### S9.3. Training-only empirical composition comparators

The empirical benchmarks comprised a query-wise comparator and six comparators with coherent paths across increasing thresholds. Each outer fit excluded the test and designated validation folds used in Text S5. The query-wise comparator used location-equal training frequencies conditional on the exact old-visible six-bit mask $m$. At each threshold, its state probabilities were

\[
q_m(s)=\frac{n_{ms}+\pi_{ms}}{n_m+1},
\]

where $n_{ms}$ is the same-mask training count and $\pi_{ms}$ is the pooled training-state frequency with a total symmetric pseudocount of one, restricted and renormalized to states containing all visible bits of $m$. Unseen masks used this compatible prior. This comparator uses training labels at each of the 16 scored thresholds, whereas the structured dependence parameters were fitted at three anchors. It is a task-level comparator with neither matched capacity nor matched fitting anchors. Its distributions are valid at each threshold, but a common monotone coupling across thresholds is not guaranteed.

For the coherent comparators, the training weights were either location-equal or jurisdiction/PWS/location-equal and were normalized to the training sample size. For each hidden analyte, the prior reporting probability was

\[
a_j(\alpha)=
\frac{\sum_{i:V_{ij}=0}w_iY_{ij}(\alpha)+0.5(1-\alpha)}
{\sum_{i:V_{ij}=0}w_i+1}.
\]

The prior $\pi_m(\alpha)$ treated hidden analytes independently with probabilities $a_j(\alpha)$ and fixed visible bits to one. The complete distribution was

\[
q_m(\alpha)=
\frac{H_m(\alpha)+\lambda\pi_m(\alpha)}{W_m+\lambda},
\]

where $H_m$ is the weighted same-mask state histogram and $W_m$ its total weight. All combinations of the two training-weight choices and $\lambda\in\{0.1,1,10\}$ were retained; $\lambda=1$ was the reference for interval comparisons, not a value selected from held-out performance. A coupling first selects the empirical or prior component once. The empirical component selects one training record; the prior uses six fixed uniform draws against nonincreasing $a_j(\alpha)$. The mixture weights are constant over thresholds, so each coordinate can only change from reported to unreported as the cutoff increases. The old-cutoff endpoint equals the retained mask.

Table S20 reports all seven empirical comparators, including the earlier query-wise comparator, and all specified reference-strength log-score contrasts. Unlike the principal fixed-margin comparison, these benchmarks change marginal as well as joint probabilities. Differences between the query-wise and coherent comparators cannot be attributed solely to coherence because their priors also differ. Intervals are pointwise, conditional on fitted predictions, and not adjusted for multiple comparisons.

### S9.4. Probability sensitivity, influence, and record pairing

The log-score decomposition was repeated with uniform mixing weights $\epsilon=10^{-8},10^{-10},10^{-12},10^{-14}$; the primary score retained $10^{-12}$. No observed reporting state had exactly zero raw probability under any of the three fixed-margin models. Each jurisdiction and each PWS was also removed in turn, with complete threshold trajectories retained and hierarchical weights recomputed. Table S19 reports the resulting sensitivity and influence ranges; these ranges are not confidence intervals. Visibility-group contributions retain full-cohort weights and differ between primary and record-equal weighting. They are distinct from the marginal input-block comparison in Table S18.

Within each outer fold, old-visible mask, and query threshold, each record's own predictive score was compared with the expected score under uniformly random reassignment of predictions and with the score of the group's mean predictive distribution. The mean distribution was constructed without outcomes. At the score level,

\[
L_{\mathrm{random}}-L_{\mathrm{own}}
=(L_{\mathrm{random}}-L_{\mathrm{pooled}})
+(L_{\mathrm{pooled}}-L_{\mathrm{own}}).
\]

The first term is nonnegative by Jensen's inequality and reflects variation among predictive distributions. It accounted for most of the random-minus-own difference for both structured models (Table S21). Random reassignment alone therefore does not isolate record-specific predictive information. The pooled distribution uses predictions generated from evaluation inputs and is a descriptive comparison, not a train-only benchmark or an independent validation set.

These diagnostics retain the post-selection status defined in Text S5; the release-vintage evaluation in Text S8 concerns the January models.
