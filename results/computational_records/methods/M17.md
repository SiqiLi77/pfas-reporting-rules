## Text S17. Follow-Up Allocation with Minimum Coverage of Initially Unreported Locations

The allocation extension used the 23,846 locations in Text S12 with complete initial 25-analyte panels and frozen restricted-input predictions. A target was a subsequent sample with at least two of 25 PFAS reported but at most one of the shared six. There were 912 targets, including 217 at locations initially reporting none of the 25 analytes. Each jurisdiction received sampling capacity equal to 20% of its eligible locations. References were random allocation, initial 25-analyte count ranking, and frozen regression ranking. The initial-count reference uses broader measurements than the regression score; it is not an equal-information comparison of algorithms.

Guarded policies gave the initially 25-unreported group capacity equal to the larger of its initial-count-ranking allocation and a specified minimum mean selection rate. Floors of 5%, 10%, 15%, and 20% corresponded to 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1 times the full-cohort budget rate. Remaining capacity was assigned among initially reported locations by initial count. Within the initially unreported group, regression ranking and random allocation were compared. Ties and fractional boundary capacity represented expected randomized allocation. A group-average floor is not an individual minimum selection probability. All floor values and 10%, 20%, and 30% total capacities were retained; no optimum was selected using evaluation outcomes.

The full floor was the primary new contrast. At 20% total capacity, it increased target recall among initially unreported locations from 5.3% under initial-count ranking to 31.1%, a paired increase of 25.9 percentage points (95% interval, 18.7–33.4). Overall recall fell from 76.0% to 38.6%, a decrease of 37.4 points (33.5–41.6). At the same full floor, ranking within the unreported group rather than selecting randomly increased overall recall by 2.65 points (0.99–4.45) and the group's recall by 11.14 points (4.20–18.73). The comparison shows a measurable tradeoff, not a preferred policy (Table S33).

Two thousand paired PWS bootstrap draws within fixed jurisdictions recalculated ranking, capacity, ties, and fractional selection with predictions held fixed. These intervals do not include model-development uncertainty. A calendar-scored subset contained 5,048 locations, 173 targets, and 34 initially unreported targets; its allocation curve is descriptive (Table S33). Nominal sampling capacity and target recall are not monetary cost, health benefit, or demonstrated operational performance. The full results distinguish retention of known patterns from follow-up coverage where no analyte was initially reported.
