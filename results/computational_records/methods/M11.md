## Text S11. Fixed Margin Count Prior Benchmark

The historical count-prior construction was re-evaluated on the final strict cohort without choosing hyperparameters from its test scores. For each outer fold, designated validation and test jurisdictions were excluded from prior estimation. At the three anchors, alpha equal to 0, 0.5, and 1, location-equal training counts estimated the seven count probabilities with 0.5 pseudocount per class. Each count probability was divided equally among the six-bit patterns of that size. Priors were interpolated logarithmically between adjacent anchors and normalized, then projected by iterative proportional fitting to the same six frozen marginal probabilities used by the primary models. Exact zero or one margins restricted the feasible support; no artificial margin clipping was used. The maximum fitted margin error was 9.80 × 10⁻¹². Projection changes named-pattern probabilities through their marginal constraints, so uniformity conditional on count is a property of the prior, not of the final distribution.

The 338,496 evaluation records comprised 16 thresholds at each of 21,156 locations in 8,316 PWSs and 56 jurisdictions. The primary weighting and 20,000 paired two-level bootstrap replicates followed Text S6. Complete-pattern negative log-likelihood decomposed exactly into count and conditional-identity losses. Table S23 shows that the detailed dependence models did not achieve resolved extra gains beyond the count-prior benchmark. Its 96.9% share of the shared-shock gain relative to independence is a ratio of point estimates, not an equivalence test or causal attribution.

Threshold-path compatibility was tested over every distinct adjacent pair of evaluated 64-state distributions. A Boolean upper set contains every pattern obtained by adding reports to a pattern already in the set. Its probability cannot increase when all reporting cutoffs rise under a common monotone realization. A maximum-weight-closure problem on the 64-state Boolean lattice, solved by floating-capacity minimum cut, found the largest upper-set probability increase at each adjacent pair. Exhaustive enumeration on smaller lattices and independent recalculation of each reported violating set checked the solver; numerical details are retained in the computational verification record.

The benchmark had no detected violation in any of its six count-tail probabilities but failed the full upper-set check at 83 of 20,355 distinct adjacent path comparisons. Those failures represented 887 location-transition instances at 338 distinct locations (1.60%). The largest probability increase was 0.00006555, or 0.00656 percentage points, exceeding the 10⁻⁸ audit tolerance. This small failure precludes describing the benchmark as strictly threshold coherent; it does not establish a scoring advantage for the structured models or invalidate a query-wise diagnostic comparison. The audit covers the evaluated grid, not every real-valued threshold position. All comparisons remain exploratory after model development and selection.
