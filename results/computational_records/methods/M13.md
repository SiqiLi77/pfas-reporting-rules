## Text S13. Information Retention across Paired Monitoring Samples

This descriptive extension used the final archive and the unchanged 26,461-location strict SE1 starting cohort. Each eligible location required one complete 25-analyte Method 533 panel at SE1 and another at the first strictly later collection date among SE2–SE4. Date selection preceded checks of report status and completeness; an invalid first later panel was not replaced by a more favorable later sample. Each panel required exactly one row per analyte, a common SampleID and date, and consistent event code, method, unit, and reporting-level metadata. The final cohort contained 25,756 locations, 10,113 PWSs, and 66 jurisdictions (Table S27, Panel A). First samples ranged from January 3, 2023 to May 18, 2026; subsequent samples ranged from March 29, 2023 to June 17, 2026. These archive collection dates extend beyond the nominal 2023–2025 program period. The median separation was 178 days, with a range of 1–1,211 days. Among the 1,904 locations initially reporting PFHxA and PFPeA together, the median interval was 168 days; the 178-day median refers to the full paired cohort. Nondetect concentrations remained missing rather than being replaced by zero.

Eight reporting conditions crossed three binary choices: native UCMR 5 versus older numeric limits for the shared six; six versus all 25 Method 533 analytes; and the first versus both observed samples. Additional analytes always used their native limits. Applying old limits to the six shared analytes while retaining 19 additional analytes is a hybrid analytical control, not a reconstruction of an actual historical monitoring program. The reference was the union of pairs co-reported within either complete 25-analyte sample at native limits. Pairs were formed within each sample before taking this union; reports from different dates were never combined to create a pair not observed together. The reference contained 3,647 locations with at least one multi-PFAS sample and 30,627 distinct location–pair occurrences. It is an observed reference, not the unobserved set of all PFAS in water.

Binary coverage divided the number of reference-positive locations still having a multi-PFAS sample by 3,647. For each of these locations, pair coverage divided the number of retained named pairs by the reference pair count at that location; the primary estimate averaged these fractions equally across locations. A separate pooled-pair estimate divided total retained location–pairs by 30,627. The two pair summaries have different weights and were not interchanged. The two-sample 25-analyte condition has coverage of one by definition. PWS-equal and jurisdiction/PWS-equal results accompany the primary location-equal estimates in the machine-readable tables. No assay costs, detection probabilities beyond the observed samples, or optimal monitoring schedule were estimated.

For each pair, conditional re-reporting was the fraction of first-sample-positive locations that co-reported the same pair in the subsequent sample. Figure S3b displays the 12 pairs with the largest first-sample support, selected before inspecting re-reporting fractions. All 300 pair results remain available, including pairs with sparse or absent support. Re-reporting does not establish continuous persistence, unchanged concentration, or shared origin. Fixed source-water categories, a 90–270-day interval restriction, and the administratively matched subset were descriptive sensitivities. A twice-native-limit sensitivity rebuilt both the reference and evaluated reports at twice the native limits, leaving 1,358 reference-positive locations (Table S28). It addresses near-limit sensitivity without assuming a measurement-error distribution.

Intervals used 2,000 paired PWS-cluster bootstrap samples within fixed jurisdiction strata, with seed 202609251. Resampling the same locations across conditions preserved their pairing. These intervals differ from the two-level jurisdiction–PWS bootstrap used for the main cross-cycle contrast. Eligibility, pair formation, and reporting flags were independently reconstructed from the raw archive; selected keys, all reporting conditions, and all pair transitions were retained. The analysis rule was locked before computation, but the broader project results were already known. These comparisons describe retained observation, not prospective validation or equal-cost experimental designs.
